Mission and History

SMART Recovery Australia is a national not-for-profit organization dedicated to helping people live a life beyond addiction through evidence-based, inclusive, and empowering programs
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The SMART approach (Self-Management and Recovery Training) originated in the United States in 1994 as a secular, science-based alternative to 12-step groups, and quickly grew into an international movement
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Key milestones include:

1994 (USA) – SMART Recovery founded to meet demand for a secular, science-based addiction support program (starting with 42 meetings, now over 2,000 in North America)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. It is grounded in Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) and other evidence-based practices, emphasizing self-empowerment rather than spiritual or religious themes
aph.gov.au
.

2004 (Australia) – SMART Recovery introduced in Australia via a pilot at St. Vincent’s Hospital in Sydney, offering the community a new recovery option grounded in science
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The success of this pilot led to rapid adoption.

2007 – SMART Recovery Australia was established as an independent non-profit organization to expand the program nationally
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. This allowed the program’s evidence-based framework to be applied to all forms of addiction (not only alcohol or drugs).

2020s – The organization experienced significant growth and innovation. By 2023, SMART Recovery Australia was partnering with over 75 health and community organizations, running around 250 weekly groups and 13,000 meetings annually, reaching over 130,000 Australians to date
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. In the last few years, expansion accelerated (details on recent developments are provided in a later section).

Today, SMART Recovery operates across 23+ countries as the world’s largest network of science-based mutual aid meetings, with Australia as a major contributor to this growth

SMART Recovery Model and Core Principles

SMART Recovery’s model is fundamentally evidence-based, drawing on established therapeutic techniques like Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) and Motivational Interviewing (MI) to promote positive change
aph.gov.au
. Rather than viewing addiction as an incurable disease, SMART Recovery emphasizes that people can gain control over their behaviors through self-awareness, rational thinking, and learned skills. The program is secular and science-driven: it does not involve religious or spiritual steps, but instead focuses on cognitive-behavioral strategies and mutual support to empower individuals. Key features of the SMART Recovery approach include promoting personal choice, accountability, and resilience in recovery
aph.gov.au
. Meetings are non-judgmental and secular, creating a welcoming atmosphere for those who may not resonate with faith-based or confrontational programs
aph.gov.au
.

The 4-Point Program: SMART Recovery’s curriculum is often summarized in a Four-Point Program, which provides a roadmap for the skills and areas of focus in recovery. The four key points are to
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
:

Building and Maintaining Motivation – Strengthening one’s desire and commitment to change. Participants clarify their own reasons for recovery and regularly reaffirm their goals.

Coping with Urges and Cravings – Learning tools to withstand urges or impulses to engage in the addictive behavior. Techniques might include urge surfing, distraction, or deconstructing the thoughts that drive cravings.

Managing Thoughts, Feelings, and Behaviors – Using CBT methods to address the thinking patterns and emotional responses that can lead to problematic behavior. For example, the ABC model (explained below) is taught to help participants challenge irrational beliefs and develop healthier responses
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Living a Balanced Life – Developing a lifestyle that is fulfilling and balanced, reducing the need for addictive escapes. This involves setting meaningful life goals, finding positive activities, repairing relationships, and maintaining good physical and mental health.

These points are not “steps” to complete sequentially, but rather ongoing areas of mastery. Meetings often revolve around practicing these skills and reviewing progress in each area. Underlying the 4-Point Program is the principle that changing one’s thinking leads to changes in feeling and behavior – a core CBT concept that SMART embraces (“we feel the way we think; it’s not the events but our beliefs about them that affect us, and by changing our thinking we can change how we feel and act”
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
).

Core Principles: SMART Recovery Australia articulates a set of guiding principles that inform how the program is delivered, especially in the group setting
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
:

Self-Management – The belief that people can take charge of their own behavior. Recovery is seen as a self-directed change process; participants are in control of their decisions and path (with facilitators and tools as supports)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Mutual Aid – Emphasis on peer support: people learn best from each other. Meetings leverage the power of group help, where participants share experiences and solutions, rather than passively receiving lectures
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Choice – Individuals choose their own goals, skills, and tools in SMART Recovery. There is no one-size approach (for instance, abstinence is not a requirement if someone’s goal is moderation)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The program supports each person’s personal recovery objectives and fosters self-determination.

Person-Centered – Lived experience is central to SMART Recovery. Many facilitators and contributors have personal experience with recovery, and the program respects individual differences. Participants are treated as equals in guiding what works for them
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Evidence-Led – All SMART Recovery methods are grounded in scientific evidence, and the program encourages ongoing evaluation and research
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. SMART constantly updates its tools based on the latest psychology of addiction and recovery research, rather than tradition or dogma.

These core principles ensure that SMART Recovery stays practical, flexible, and respectful of each participant. For example, the program explicitly avoids labels like “addict” and does not ask participants to admit powerlessness; instead, it focuses on building self-efficacy. The ultimate goal is to empower participants with tangible skills so they can manage urges, handle life’s challenges, and pursue their values without reliance on addictive substances or behaviors
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
aph.gov.au
.

Meeting Structure and Facilitation Process

SMART Recovery meetings follow a consistent structure and set of guidelines, creating a safe, collaborative environment where participants can work on their recovery goals. Meetings typically last 60–90 minutes and are held once per week (though individuals may attend multiple meetings per week if they wish)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Each meeting is led by a trained facilitator who keeps the group on track and introduces SMART tools, but the atmosphere is informal and peer-driven – everyone has a chance to speak and contribute. All meetings are free to attend and open to the public (some may be designated for specific populations as noted earlier).

Typical Meeting Format: SMART Recovery uses a standard meeting agenda so that participants know what to expect. The format has four main components
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
:

Check-In – At the start, each participant is invited to briefly share how their past week has been, especially regarding successes or challenges in managing their addictive behavior. This “check-in” allows people to report progress on their action plans from the previous meeting and identify any pressing issue they want to discuss. It also helps everyone get acquainted and provides immediate peer support or congratulations for milestones
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Discussion (Agenda Setting & Working Time) – After check-ins, the group engages in a focused discussion. Participants propose agenda items – typically a problem they’re facing, a craving that came up, a tool they want to practice, or a topic (like dealing with anger or managing social situations). The facilitator guides the group in addressing each agenda item using CBT-based tools and techniques. This might involve doing a worksheet exercise (e.g. an ABC analysis of a recent trigger), a group brainstorm of solutions, or role-playing a challenging scenario. Everyone can contribute ideas, drawing on their experiences (this is the mutual-aid in action)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The facilitator ensures the discussion stays constructive and focused on practical strategies rather than war stories or purely venting.

Sharing Solutions (Mutual Aid) – As part of the discussion, there is an emphasis on sharing solutions and coping methods that have worked. Participants learn from one another – for example, someone might share how they got through an urge by going for a run, or how they used deep breathing to manage anxiety instead of drinking
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. This fosters a sense of camaraderie and shows that everyone can both receive and give help. The facilitator may introduce or reinforce specific SMART Recovery tools (like the Cost-Benefit Analysis, Change Plan, hierarchy of values, etc.) relevant to the issues raised.

Check-Out (Closing & Plan) – In the last part of the meeting, each person is encouraged to articulate a plan for the next week
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. This “check-out” involves stating one or two concrete actions or goals they will work on before the next meeting (for example, “I will attend the gym twice and call a friend instead of gambling online,” or “I’ll practice an urge surfing technique when cravings hit”). This helps translate the meeting insights into real-life change. The facilitator may summarize the key takeaways and remind everyone of upcoming meetings or resources. Meetings end on a positive, forward-looking note, reinforcing motivation.

Throughout this process, trained facilitators play a crucial role in maintaining the structure and supportive tone of the meeting. A SMART facilitator is not a lecturer or therapist, but rather a guide and moderator. Facilitators ensure that the meeting follows SMART’s principles and that everyone gets a chance to participate. They help keep the discussion solution-focused and prevent any single person from dominating. Importantly, facilitators do not tell participants what to do or provide all the answers – instead, they prompt the group with questions and encourage members to apply the tools themselves
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. For instance, if someone asks, “What should I do about my situation?”, a facilitator might redirect: “What do others think? Has anyone faced something similar?” or “Let’s try an ABC on that scenario together.” This empowers the group and keeps the meeting collaborative.

Group Agreement and Safe Environment: SMART Recovery meetings operate under a set of simple ground rules to protect the safety and comfort of all participants
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. These guidelines, often posted or stated at the start of meetings, include:

Confidentiality – “What is said in the meeting stays in the meeting.” Participants’ privacy is respected so they can share openly
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Facilitators reinforce that personal details should not be discussed outside the group.

No “drug talk” or War Stories – Participants are asked to avoid glamorizing or in-depth using stories that might trigger others
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The focus should remain on recovery and solutions rather than explicit drug/alcohol descriptions.

One Person Talking at a Time – Basic meeting etiquette to ensure everyone is heard without interruptions
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Cross-talk is minimized; people can respond once someone has finished sharing.

Respect and Non-Judgment – All attendees are treated with respect regardless of their background or the nature of their addiction
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. There is no shaming for relapses or coercing others; the atmosphere is accepting and empathetic.

No Intoxication – Participants are expected to attend meetings sober and not under the influence, for the safety of the group
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. If someone does attend intoxicated, they may be asked to leave and come back next time sober (with concern for their well-being).

Stay for the Whole Meeting – Members are encouraged to stay until the end (no coming and going), to minimize disruption and ensure everyone hears the closing planning section
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

No Children in Meetings – Given the sensitive topics, meetings are adults-only unless specifically designated (this is to ensure everyone can speak freely)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Phones Off – Mobile phones should be silenced to avoid distractions
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

These guidelines help create a safe, inclusive space where participants can trust the process and each other
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The facilitator’s job is to gently enforce these rules – for example, stopping side conversations, or reminding someone to avoid explicit details if they stray into “drug talk”.

Facilitator Principles and Ethics: SMART Recovery Australia holds its facilitators to a high standard of ethics and competence. Facilitators may be professionals (like counselors, social workers, healthcare workers) or lay volunteers (including people in recovery themselves or concerned others) who have undergone the SMART training. Regardless of background, all facilitators agree to a Code of Conduct and guiding principles that mirror the program’s values. Some key facilitator guidelines include:

Promote Participant Independence: The facilitator’s role is to help participants gain independence from addictive behavior, not to create dependency on meetings or on the facilitator. They encourage attendees to practice self-management skills and make their own choices, reinforcing the idea that each person is in charge of their recovery
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Act as a Community Resource: Facilitators aim to make SMART Recovery available to their local community, often networking with other health services. They serve as ambassadors of the program, raising awareness and ensuring meetings are accessible to those who need them
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Respect Dignity and Diversity: Each participant is treated with care, respect, and without judgment, honoring differences in culture, ethnicity, gender, age, sexual orientation, and so on
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Facilitators cultivate an environment where all feel welcome and understood.

Maintain Integrity and Boundaries: Facilitators are expected to act with honesty and integrity, both inside and outside the meeting
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. They must avoid conflicts of interest or exploiting the group for personal, religious, political, or business purposes
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Any form of harassment or inappropriate relations with participants is strictly prohibited
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Essentially, facilitators prioritize the welfare of participants above any personal agenda.

Protect Confidentiality: Upholding privacy is critical. Facilitators never divulge personal information shared in the group, and they also coach participants to respect each other’s confidentiality
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. They only break confidentiality in extreme situations (e.g. if someone is at risk of imminent harm, in which case a facilitator might seek emergency help, following SMART’s safety protocols)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Encourage Self-Determination: Rather than prescribing goals, facilitators support participants’ own recovery goals and self-determination
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. If someone’s goals lie outside the scope of what SMART can offer (for instance, if a participant needs medical detox or specialized therapy), facilitators will refer or suggest seeking additional help – always respecting the participant’s right to choose
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Continuous Learning: Facilitators are expected to pursue knowledge and competence in the SMART tools and concepts
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. They regularly update their skills (through refresher trainings, reading research, etc.) so they can effectively guide meetings. They also acknowledge if something is beyond their scope – they are not expected to have all the answers, especially on topics outside addiction recovery. In such cases, facilitators steer the conversation back to the group or suggest professional support elsewhere
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Collaborative Attitude: Facilitators work cooperatively with other local services and acknowledge the value of multiple pathways to recovery
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. SMART does not position itself as “the only way,” and facilitators may encourage participants to use any additional supports that help (e.g. therapy, other peer groups, etc.).

By adhering to these principles, SMART Recovery facilitators create a trustworthy and empowering group process. The result is a meeting environment where participants feel supported, not judged, and where they can openly discuss challenges and successes as peers. A participant from Perth describes the experience: “The CBT-based focus of SMART has helped me remain abstinent by assisting me to identify strategies to combat cravings and triggers, and to develop plans to deal with troubling situations as they arise”
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. This encapsulates how a well-run SMART meeting, under skilled facilitation, can equip individuals with practical coping methods and hope for living a balanced life.

Tools and Resources for Recovery

One of SMART Recovery’s strengths is the variety of practical tools and educational resources it provides to both participants and facilitators. These tools are grounded in cognitive-behavioral techniques and are designed to be easy to learn and apply in everyday life. Many are available as worksheets, handouts, or interactive exercises during meetings, and also for personal use between meetings. Below are some of the key resources:

SMART Recovery Toolbox: An assortment of evidence-based exercises and worksheets often collectively referred to as the 4-Point Toolkit. This includes tools like the ABC Model (to analyze Activating events, Beliefs, Consequences), Change Plan Worksheets (for setting goals and action steps), Cost–Benefit Analysis (examining pros and cons of using vs. quitting), Trigger Logs and Coping Strategies, Problem Solving sheets, and more. For example, the ABC Model is frequently used to help participants challenge and reframe the thoughts that lead to emotional upsets or lapses: by identifying irrational beliefs and disputing them, individuals can learn to respond to triggers more rationally (“change the way we think to change how we feel”)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. These tools are introduced in meetings and often provided as take-home exercises. The SMART Recovery website hosts a library of these worksheets and guidelines, which are freely accessible
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

SMART Recovery Handbook/Manuals: SMART Recovery International publishes a handbook that compiles the program’s core concepts, tools, and recovery strategies. In Australia, participants can obtain the SMART Recovery Manual (often as part of treatment programs or via the website). This serves as a self-help book to reinforce what is learned in meetings, with chapters on each of the 4 Points, motivational tips, and personal exercises. There are also specialized manuals/toolkits, such as a Youth Toolkit and a Family & Friends Toolkit, tailored to those audiences
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. (Notably, a special edition of the SMART Handbook was even created for Aboriginal communities, reflecting a commitment to cultural relevance.)

Digital Tools – SMART Track Mobile App: To extend support beyond the meetings, SMART Recovery Australia launched a mobile application called SMART Track. This app (available for iOS and Android) is essentially “recovery in your pocket.” It allows users to track their progress and cravings over time by answering weekly questions, yielding tailored feedback and personal recovery statistics
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Users can set their own recovery goals within the app, record their sources of motivation, and monitor changes in their behavior or mood

. The app provides on-demand tips, exercises, and reminders corresponding to SMART tools to help users stay on course between meetings
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au

Both participants and facilitators have praised the app – facilitators note that “participants can stay on track in between meetings, accessing the app after hours, any time they wish”, making support available 24/7 in a self-directed manner

. The development of SMART Track was funded by a health innovation grant, and it was created in consultation with leading addiction researchers and the SMART community

Online Resource Center: The SMART Recovery Australia website offers a rich resource hub for anyone to explore. Under “Explore Resources,” one can find links to all the major tools and guides

In summary, SMART Recovery Australia equips both participants and facilitators with a robust set of tools. This wealth of resources underpins why SMART Recovery is considered an “empowerment-based” program: it not only provides moral support, but also the know-how for individuals to self-manage their addictive behavior one day at a time.

Facilitator Training and Certification

All SMART Recovery meetings are led by certified facilitators, and SMART Recovery Australia runs an extensive training program to prepare these facilitators. The training is open to a wide range of people – from professionals in the alcohol and drug (AOD) field, to healthcare workers, to peer volunteers or anyone interested in running a SMART group (including those from schools, prisons, or community organizations). The overarching goal is to ensure facilitators are well-versed in SMART’s tools and group facilitation techniques, and to maintain fidelity to the program’s evidence-based approach.

Trainees practice these tools themselves (e.g., filling out an ABC worksheet on a hypothetical scenario) to grasp how they work in practice.

Facilitation Techniques: This is a major focus – how to facilitate discussion rather than lecturing, how to engage participants with open-ended questions, how to handle an overly talkative member or a very quiet group, etc. The training emphasizes creating a safe, non-judgmental space and guiding a group conversation that is participant-driven
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Trainees learn how to do the check-in/checkout, how to elicit agenda items, and how to gently correct if someone breaks a guideline (for example, how to interrupt “war stories” tactfully and refocus the group).

Scenarios and Problem-Solving: Through role-plays, trainees encounter common challenges (like someone coming in intoxicated, or someone asking for advice the facilitator can’t give, or conflict between participants) and learn strategies to address them consistent with SMART principles. They also cover what to do in cases of disclosures of harm (e.g., if someone is suicidal or reports child abuse – understanding mandatory reporting duties and when to involve SMART HQ or other services)
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.

Ethics and Self-Care: The code of conduct is reviewed, stressing boundaries, confidentiality, and professionalism. Facilitators are also encouraged to practice self-care and seek support if they find the role emotionally taxing (this is especially highlighted for peer facilitators who may be in recovery themselves).

To become certified, trainees typically must attend all sessions and demonstrate their understanding (there may be a short knowledge quiz or group presentation at the end). There is no formal exam, but trainers observe each person’s participation and often provide coaching until the trainee is meeting the competencies. Because class sizes are small, there is plenty of individual feedback.  – reflecting the growing demand for SMART meetings across different sectors. Many organizations send their staff for certification so they can offer SMART on-site. Meeting Access: Online and In-Person Options

SMART Recovery Australia offers flexible access to its support groups, with both in-person and online meetings available to suit different needs and circumstances
aph.gov.au
. This hybrid model ensures that no matter where someone lives or what their schedule is, they can benefit from SMART’s program.

Face-to-Face Meetings: There are hundreds of in-person SMART meetings held in communities across every state and territory. These typically take place in venues like community health centers, non-profit organizations, church halls, treatment clinics, or even libraries – any accessible location where a small group can gather. One can find meetings in major cities (e.g. Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, etc.) as well as in regional towns. In-person meetings offer the benefit of direct human connection and are often a good fit for those who prefer traditional group settings. Meeting schedules are generally weekly on a set day/time (for example, every Tuesday at 6pm in a given location). Some organizations host multiple SMART meetings at different times (morning, lunchtime, evening) to accommodate varying availabilities. All meetings are listed on the SMART Recovery Australia meeting finder website. Attending is free and usually no prior booking is required – you can simply show up at the scheduled time. However, some meetings that occur inside facilities (like a rehab center or prison) might be closed to the general public, whereas most community meetings are open to all.

Online Meetings: Even before 2020, SMART Australia had a few online meetings, but the offering expanded dramatically and has now become a permanent, robust feature. Online SMART meetings are conducted via videoconferencing (commonly Zoom). Participants can join from anywhere in the country (or world), which is especially valuable for those in remote areas or those unable to attend in person due to health, mobility, or scheduling constraints. Online meetings are offered every day of the week at various times. For instance, one might find a Monday evening online meeting, a Wednesday midday meeting, etc., ensuring there is likely a session that fits one’s schedule. To attend, individuals typically register online through the SMART website – they choose a meeting time and receive the Zoom link and a password. SMART online meetings adhere to the same format and guidelines as face-to-face ones, and they are run by trained facilitators who have additional training in managing virtual group dynamics. Participants may choose whether or not to use their webcam; SMART online meetings are accommodating in that regard, and also provide a chat box for those who prefer to type responses
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. (SMART notes that while video participation is encouraged for engagement, it is not mandatory, and many meetings allow flexibility in mode of participation
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
.) Importantly, these online sessions are nationwide, bringing together a rich mix of people. During the peak of COVID-19, SMART Australia went from 6 online meetings to nearly 100 online meetings, backed by government grants
recoveryanswers.org
recoveryanswers.org
. Many of those remain ongoing, and SMART continues to support facilitators in hosting virtual meetings as an integral part of their services.

Meeting Finder and Scheduling: SMART Recovery Australia provides an easy online meeting locator tool (via their website) where users can search for meetings by location or filter for online meetings
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. The interface shows the day, time, format (online vs in-person), and any special notes (e.g. “Women’s meeting” or “Youth-focused meeting”) for each listing. This database is kept up-to-date, especially now with the Facilitator Portal enabling real-time updates
smartrecoveryaustralia.com.au
. Users can also call or email SMART Recovery Australia for help finding a suitable meeting. With the current scale (400+ meetings weekly), essentially there is a SMART meeting happening at almost any given time on the calendar, particularly when including international options from other regions (the site can redirect to SMART meetings in other countries if someone needs a different time zone). SMART’s global network means if an Australian can’t find a late-night meeting locally, they might join a meeting in the UK or USA morning time, etc., though generally the Australian branch has built a comprehensive schedule covering early morning through late evening in local time.

Consistency and Drop-In Model: Whether online or offline, SMART meetings operate on a drop-in basis. There is no formal enrollment or waitlist – individuals can start attending at any time, and attend as many or as few meetings as they want. Some attend weekly for years; others come for a few weeks to get back on track. Newcomers are welcome at every meeting and will receive an introduction. There is also no cost (though in-person meetings might pass a hat for optional gold coin donations to cover room rent or refreshments, and online meetings might suggest a small donation to support the program, but it’s voluntary). The scheduling is very user-friendly: for online meetings, once registered, you’ll get reminder emails; for in-person, you just note the schedule and attend. If an in-person meeting is canceled (e.g. due to a public holiday), the online schedule usually notes it, and often an alternative nearby meeting or an online session can be used that week.

The combination of in-person and online options means SMART Recovery can reach people regardless of geography. For example, someone in a rural area with no local meeting can still join an online group and not feel alone. Conversely, someone who craves face-to-face connection can likely find a meeting in their region, given the extensive spread of groups (especially with the growth into new areas recently). Many participants actually choose to do both: they might attend their local in-person meeting and also hop into an extra online meeting for additional support or if they miss their regular one. All of this embodies SMART Recovery Australia’s commitment to accessibility – ensuring support is available whenever and wherever it's needed
