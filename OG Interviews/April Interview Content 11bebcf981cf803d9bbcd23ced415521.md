# **Key reflections from April’s interview**

1. SMART Recovery creates safe spaces through facilitators with lived experience, focusing on peer support and sharing ideas rather than giving advice.
2. SMART offers various programs, including youth-specific meetings, family and friends meetings, Yarn Smart meetings for Indigenous communities, and Inside Out programs for those in correctional facilities.
3. SMART Recovery takes a holistic approach, addressing multiple behaviors and underlying issues rather than focusing on specific addictions.
4. The organisation faces challenges in public awareness, with many people unaware of alternatives to 12-step programs.
5. April emphasises the importance of connection in recovery, stating that "the opposite of addiction isn't sobriety, it's connection."
6. SMART Recovery aims to expand from 350 to 1,000 meetings, focusing on partnerships, collaboration, and innovation.
7. The organisation is working to make recovery accessible to everyone, including neurodivergent individuals and justice-involved people.
8. April acknowledges the courage and resilience of participants and facilitators over the past 20 years.
9. SMART Recovery has been largely community-funded and is now working with policymakers to ensure peer support is recognised as part of the continuum of care.
10. April emphasises the need to focus on people rather than problems, highlighting the strength and resilience of those in recovery.

**Reflection on April's concept of the SMART Stories project:**

Based on April's responses, the SMART Stories project appears to be a photography-based initiative aimed at humanising recovery and challenging stigma. April envisions it as a way to showcase the everyday aspects of recovery, focusing on the "four Cs" of the Yarn Smart program: Culture, Community, Connection, and Country.

The project seems designed to shift the narrative away from addiction as a personal failing or lack of willpower, instead highlighting the strength, resilience, and humanity of those in recovery. April suggests that the photos will capture ordinary moments - a cup of coffee, family interactions, connections to nature - that represent the real, human face of recovery.

By showcasing these everyday scenes and personal stories, the project aims to challenge societal perceptions of addiction and recovery. It seeks to demonstrate that recovery is an ongoing, human process deeply connected to one's community, culture, and daily life. This approach aligns with SMART Recovery's broader mission of destigmatizing addiction and promoting a more holistic, empowering approach to recovery.
