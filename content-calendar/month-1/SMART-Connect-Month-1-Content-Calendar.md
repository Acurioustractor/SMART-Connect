**Forum:** Resources & Tools
**Posted by:** Community Manager

**Body:**

**Resource: 20 Check-In Questions for SMART Meetings**

Check-ins help participants settle in and connect. But it's easy to get stuck asking the same question every week.

**Here are 20 alternatives to "How was your week?"**

1. What's one thing you're looking forward to this week?
2. What's taking up space in your mind right now?
3. If you could give your week a weather report, what would it be?
4. What's one small win from this week?
5. What brought you here today?
6. What's something you're grateful for right now?
7. What's one word to describe how you're feeling?
8. What's a challenge you're currently navigating?
9. What's something you learned recently?
10. What's one thing you'd like to get out of today's meeting?
11. What's been easier or harder than expected this week?
12. What's something you're proud of (big or small)?
13. What's a question you're sitting with right now?
14. How are you taking care of yourself lately?
15. What's something you need support with?
16. What's changed for you since the last meeting?
17. What's one thing you're working on in your recovery/wellbeing?
18. If this week was a movie, what would the title be?
19. What's bringing you energy? What's draining it?
20. What's present for you right now?

---

**How to use these:**
- Pick 1-2 per meeting (not all 20!)
- Adapt to your group's culture
- Notice which ones land well and use those more

**Have a favorite check-in question?** Share it in the comments 👇

**Introducing: Communities of Practice 🎯**

Facilitating SMART Recovery has universal challenges (e.g., "How do I handle [common issue]?").

But it also has **specific challenges** depending on who you're facilitating for:

- Family & Friends facilitators navigate different dynamics than general meetings
- Justice system facilitators face unique institutional constraints
- LGBTQIA+ facilitators create space for specific identities
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander facilitators adapt SMART for cultural context
- Youth facilitators work with different developmental needs
- Online facilitators manage screen fatigue and digital engagement

**That's what Communities of Practice are for.**

---

**What is a Community of Practice?**

A dedicated space for facilitators of a specific meeting type or population to:
- Share challenges and solutions specific to your context
- Connect with peers who "get it"
- Build resources tailored to your meetings
- Learn from each other's adaptations

---

**Current Communities of Practice:**

🏳️‍🌈 **LGBTQIA+ Facilitators**
👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 **Family & Friends Facilitators**

🌏 **Regional Hubs** (Connect with facilitators in your state/territory)
💻 **Online Meeting Facilitators**

---

**How to use Communities of Practice:**

**What makes facilitating LGBTQIA+ meetings unique:**

- Navigating complex relationships (e.g., addiction in queer community contexts)

### 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family & Friends Facilitators CoP

**What makes facilitating Family & Friends meetings unique:**
- Participants aren't "in recovery" themselves—they're supporting someone who is
- Managing guilt, blame, codependency, and boundary issues
- Holding space for grief, anger, and helplessness
- Balancing empathy with empowerment ("You can't control their recovery")

### ⚖️ Justice System Facilitators CoP

**What makes facilitating justice system meetings unique:**
- Working within institutional constraints (court mandates, reporting requirements)
- Building trust when participants are there involuntarily
- Navigating power dynamics (facilitator as extension of "the system"?)
- Adapting SMART when choice and empowerment are limited

---

### 🌟 Youth Facilitators CoP

**What makes facilitating youth meetings unique:**
- Developmental differences (adolescent brain, identity formation, peer influence)
- Adapting language and tools for younger participants
- Parental involvement (when helpful, when harmful)
- Holding space for complex family dynamics and trauma

**Forum:** Resources & Tools
**Posted by:** Community Manager

**Body:**

**Resource: Scripts for Challenging Situations in Meetings**

Every facilitator encounters challenging moments. Here are scripts you can adapt:

---

**1. Participant dominates the conversation**

> "Thanks for sharing, [Name]. I want to make sure we hear from others too. [Other participant], what's your experience with this?"

Or:

> "I'm noticing we're running short on time. Let's pause here and give space for others to share."

---

**2. Participant shares graphic/triggering content**

> "Thank you for trusting us with that. I want to pause here and remind everyone that detailed stories about [substance use/trauma/etc.] can be triggering. Let's focus on how you're coping now or what support you need, rather than details of what happened."

---

**3. Participant is highly emotional/in crisis**

> "I can see you're going through something really difficult right now. I'm glad you're here. Let's check in one-on-one after the meeting to make sure you have the support you need. For now, let's hear from others in the group—anyone else navigating something tough this week?"

---

**4. Participant challenges SMART principles**

> "That's a different perspective, and you're welcome to hold it. SMART Recovery is based on [principle]. If that doesn't resonate with you, that's okay—there are other approaches that might fit better. But in this meeting, we focus on SMART tools."

---

**5. Participant is disruptive or disrespectful**

> "I'm going to pause here. Our community agreements include [respect/confidentiality/etc.]. What just happened doesn't align with that. Let's reset."

If behavior continues:

> "I need to ask you to step out of this meeting. We can talk one-on-one afterward, but I need to maintain safety for everyone here."

---

**6. Silence after you ask a question**

> [Wait 5-10 seconds—resist filling the space]
>
> "Take your time. Sometimes these questions need a moment to sit with."

Or:

> "Let me ask it differently: [rephrase question]"

---

**7. Participant asks you a question you don't know the answer to**

> "I don't know the answer to that. Let me find out and get back to you."

Or:

> "Great question. Does anyone in the group have experience with this?"

---

**How to use these scripts:**
- Adapt them to your voice and group culture
- Practice saying them out loud so they feel natural
- Remember: You don't need to be perfect—just present and clear

**What other scenarios would be helpful to have scripts for?** Let us know below 👇

## Facilitation Notes for Community Manager

### Pacing
- **Week 1:** High activity (launch energy)
- **Week 2:** Steady rhythm (establishing patterns)
- **Week 3:** Expansion (CoPs launch)
- **Week 4:** Reflection (consolidation before Month 2)

### Response Strategy
- **Staff response time:** Under 4 hours during business hours
- **Encourage peer responses:** Wait 2 hours before staff answers questions
- **Highlight peer contributions:** Quote and thank facilitators who help others
- **Model vulnerability:** Share struggles, not just successes

### Red Flags to Watch For
- **Week 1:** Low engagement (fewer than 15 facilitators posting)
- **Week 2:** Only staff responding to questions (peers not engaging)
- **Week 3:** Communities of Practice feel empty/forced
- **Week 4:** Founding Facilitators dropping off

If you see red flags, adjust immediately. See troubleshooting guide in SMART-Connect-Week-1-Launch-Content.md.

### Green Lights (Things Going Well)
- Peer-to-peer exchanges happening without staff prompting
- Facilitators thanking each other
- "Me too" moments (people realizing they're not alone)
- Resources being saved and used
- Aboriginal facilitators engaging in Mob Yarning Space (even if quietly)
