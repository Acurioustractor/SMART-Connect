## Role Overview

### What is a Community Champion?

Community Champions are **experienced facilitators** who volunteer 3-5 hours per month to:
- Welcome new community members
- Answer questions from their experience
- Share resources and facilitation tips
- Model vulnerability and authentic connection
- Help identify what the community needs

**They are NOT:**
- Moderators (that's staff's job)
- SMART Recovery experts who must know everything
- Responsible for keeping the community active
- Required to be online daily

**Why this role matters:**
- Peer-to-peer support is more credible than staff-led content
- Facilitators trust other facilitators who've "been there"
- Champions help scale community support beyond staff capacity
- They bring diverse voices and experiences to the community

## Recruitment Strategy

### How Many Community Champions?

**Target:** 8-12 Champions (Year 1)

**Why this number:**
- Enough to provide coverage without overwhelming coordination
- Small enough to build genuine relationships
- Allows for 1-2 Champions per specialty area
- Buffer for Champions who need to step back temporarily

### When to Recruit

**Phase 1: Launch Champions (Month 1)**
- Recruit 3-4 Champions from Founding Facilitators
- These are your "early adopters" who help set the tone

**Phase 2: Growth Champions (Month 4)**
- Recruit 3-4 more Champions based on community gaps
- Look for diversity in meeting types, regions, demographics

**Phase 3: Sustainability Champions (Month 7+)**
- Recruit 2-4 Champions to replace those who step back
- Ongoing rolling recruitment

### Where to Find Champions

**Start with:**
1. **Founding Facilitators** who are highly engaged in Weeks 1-4
2. **Active contributors** who consistently help others
3. **Facilitators from underrepresented communities:**
   - Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander facilitators
   - LGBTQIA+ facilitators
   - Justice system facilitators
   - Regional/rural facilitators
   - Youth facilitators

**Red flags (do NOT recruit):**
- Facilitators who dominate conversations
- Those who position themselves as "experts" over peers
- Facilitators with unresolved complaints or concerns
- Anyone who doesn't respect cultural safety protocols

## Selection Criteria

### Essential Qualities

✅ **Been facilitating for 6+ months** (has real experience to share)
✅ **Active in the community** (posts, comments, or answers questions)
✅ **Respectful tone** (kind, non-judgmental, assumes good intent)
✅ **Cultural safety aware** (especially for Aboriginal Champions)
✅ **Can commit 3-5 hours per month** (realistic about capacity)
✅ **Models vulnerability** (shares struggles, not just wins)

### Desirable but Not Essential

- Has facilitated multiple meeting types
- Active on other online communities
- Experienced with mentoring new facilitators
- Tech-comfortable (but not required)

### Diversity Priorities

Aim for Champions who represent:
- **Meeting types:** General, Family & Friends, Justice System, Youth, Online
- **Regions:** Metro, regional, rural, remote
- **Demographics:** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander, LGBTQIA+, culturally diverse
- **Experience levels:** Mix of 1-2 year and 5+ year facilitators
- **Time zones:** Coverage across Australia (for asynchronous community)

## Role Description

### Community Champion Role Description

**Time commitment:** 3-5 hours per month (flexible)
**Term:** 6 months (renewable)
**Stipend:** $100/month (paid quarterly: $300)
**Reports to:** Community Manager

---

### Primary Responsibilities

**1. Welcome & Connection (1-2 hours/month)**
- Welcome new facilitators who join the community
- Respond to introduction posts with personal welcomes
- Share your story to model vulnerability

**2. Peer Support (2-3 hours/month)**
- Answer questions based on your facilitation experience
- Share resources, tips, and what's working in your meetings
- Participate in discussions (especially in your specialty area)
- Be present in the community 2-3 times per week (even if just reading)

**3. Community Feedback (30 minutes/month)**
- Share what you're noticing: What's working? What's missing?
- Help identify gaps in resources or support
- Participate in monthly Champion check-in call (optional but encouraged)

---

### Specific Activities

**Weekly (15-20 minutes, 2-3x per week):**
- Check community forums
- Respond to 1-2 questions or posts
- Welcome any new facilitators

**Monthly (1 hour):**
- Share one resource, tip, or reflection
- Participate in Champion check-in call (optional)
- Provide feedback to Community Manager

**Quarterly (30 minutes):**
- Reflect on what's working and what needs adjustment
- Renewal conversation with Community Manager

---

### What Success Looks Like

You'll know you're succeeding when:
- New facilitators feel welcomed and less alone
- Questions get answered by peers (not just staff)
- Other facilitators say "thank you" for your contributions
- You feel connected to other Champions
- The role energizes you more than it drains you

**Important:** If the role starts feeling like a burden, tell us immediately. We'll adjust or help you step back.

---

### Boundaries & Scope

**You ARE responsible for:**
- ✅ Sharing your experience authentically
- ✅ Welcoming and supporting peers
- ✅ Modeling respectful, kind communication
- ✅ Flagging concerning posts to Community Manager

**You are NOT responsible for:**
- ❌ Being online every day
- ❌ Knowing everything about SMART Recovery
- ❌ Moderating conflicts (that's staff's job)
- ❌ Responding to every single post
- ❌ Keeping the community "active" (not your job)
- ❌ Managing technical issues

---

### Support You'll Receive

- **Onboarding:** 60-minute orientation with Community Manager
- **Check-ins:** Monthly Champion calls (optional)
- **Direct access:** Community Manager's direct email/phone
- **Peer support:** Private Champions-only space
- **Stipend:** $100/month to honor your time
- **Recognition:** Public acknowledgment as Community Champion
- **Flexibility:** Step back anytime, no questions asked

- Clarify any concerns

**Frequency:** Monthly (same time/day each month)

**2. Community Pulse (15 min)**

- What gaps are you seeing?

**3. Coordination (10 min)**
- Upcoming community events or launches

- Cultural safety reminders
- Handling tricky situations

**5. Questions & Support (10 min)**
- Champions ask questions

**Close:**
- Appreciation for their work
- Reminder: reach out anytime between calls

### Stepping Back Gracefully

**When a Champion needs to step back:**

**1. Acknowledge without guilt**

"Thank you for being honest about your capacity. That's exactly the kind of integrity we value."

**2. Celebrate their impact**

"During your [X months] as Champion, you [specific contributions]. That matters."

**3. Final stipend payment**

Pay pro-rated amount for their final month.

**4. Public recognition** (with their permission)

Post in community thanking them for their service.

**5. Stay connected**

"You're always welcome back. And you're still a valued member of this community, Champion badge or not."

**6. Exit interview** (optional)

"What did you learn? What would you change? What advice for future Champions?"

---

### Removing a Champion (Rare)

**When necessary** (behavior issues, complaints, breach of guidelines):

**Process:**

1. **Private conversation** with Community Manager + Cultural Safety Moderator (if cultural safety issue)
2. **Clear explanation** of concern and impact
3. **Opportunity to respond** and clarify
4. **Decision:** Warning, adjusted role, or removal
5. **If removal:** Handle with dignity—no public shaming
6. **Final stipend payment:** Pay for work completed

**Communication:**

"This role isn't working out for [reasons]. We're going to remove your Champion status. You can still participate in the community as a member, but we need someone in this role who can [expectation]. Thank you for your contributions so far."

## Troubleshooting Guide

### Issue: Champion is overwhelmed

**Signs:**
- Not checking in regularly
- Responses become terse or stressed
- Expresses guilt about not doing enough

**Action:**
1. **Immediate check-in:** "I'm noticing [pattern]. How are you doing?"
2. **Reduce expectations:** "What if you just focused on [one thing]?"
3. **Offer break:** "Take 2 weeks off—no questions asked"
4. **Reframe success:** "You're not failing. The role might need adjusting."

---

### Issue: Champion is dominating

**Signs:**
- Commenting on every single post
- Other facilitators stop contributing when Champion is active
- Tone becomes prescriptive ("you should do this")

**Action:**
1. **Private conversation:** "I love your engagement, but I'm worried other voices are getting crowded out"
2. **Redirect energy:** "What if you focused on welcoming new facilitators and let others answer general questions?"
3. **Model restraint:** "Sometimes the most helpful thing is to wait and see who else responds"

---

### Issue: Champion isn't active enough

**Signs:**
- Hasn't checked in for 2+ weeks
- No posts or comments in a month
- Not attending check-in calls

**Action:**
1. **Friendly check-in:** "Haven't seen you around—everything okay?"
2. **Clarify expectations:** "We need at least 2-3 check-ins per week. Is that still doable?"
3. **Offer adjustment:** "Would a different time commitment work better?"
4. **If ongoing:** "It seems like now isn't the right time. Let's pause your role and revisit in [timeframe]."

---

### Issue: Champion gives incorrect information

**Signs:**
- Answers about SMART tools/process are incorrect
- Advice contradicts SMART principles
- Cultural safety breach

**Action:**
1. **Immediate correction** (publicly if information is harmful)
2. **Private conversation:** "Thanks for trying to help, but [correction]. Here's the accurate info."
3. **Provide resources:** "If you're unsure, here's where to check or who to tag"
4. **If repeated:** Consider whether they're the right fit for the role

---

### Issue: Champion experiences burnout

**Signs:**
- Used to be enthusiastic, now feels like a chore
- Expresses resentment about time commitment
- Quality of engagement declines

**Action:**
1. **Immediate intervention:** "Let's hit pause. What's going on?"
2. **No guilt:** "This role should energize you, not drain you"
3. **Break or exit:** "Take a month off" or "Let's transition you out gracefully"
4. **Learn:** "What would have prevented this?" (adjust for future Champions)

## Final Notes for Community Manager

### Selecting Champions

**Quality over quantity.** Better to have 4 excellent Champions than 10 mediocre ones.

**Diversity matters.** Ensure Champions reflect the diversity of facilitators you serve.

**Cultural safety is non-negotiable.** If you're recruiting an Aboriginal Champion, ensure Cultural Safety Moderator is involved in recruitment and onboarding.

---

### Supporting Champions

**Check in regularly.** Don't wait for them to say they're struggling.

**Celebrate their impact.** Small recognition goes a long way.

**Model boundaries.** If you're not taking breaks, they won't either.

**Adjust quickly.** If something isn't working, change it. Don't wait 6 months.

---

### When to Recruit More Champions

**Green lights:**
- Current Champions are thriving
- Community is growing steadily
- You have capacity to onboard and support new Champions
- There are clear gaps in coverage (meeting types, regions, etc.)

**Red lights:**
- Current Champions are struggling
- You're overwhelmed as Community Manager
- Community engagement is declining
- You don't have budget for stipends

---
