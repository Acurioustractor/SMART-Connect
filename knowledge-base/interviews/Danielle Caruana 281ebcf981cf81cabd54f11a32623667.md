Summary

**Key Themes:**

**Cultural Safety as Foundation**: Strong emphasis on creating culturally safe spaces for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander facilitators and community members. Platform must embed cultural protocols, not just add them as an afterthought.

**Community-Led Connection**: Deep interest in connecting with other Aboriginal facilitators across Australia doing family and friends work. Currently feels isolated from peers who understand both the tools AND the cultural adaptation required to make them work for mob.

**Practical Resources with Cultural Lens**: Values evidence-based SMART tools (PIUS, CRAFT, problem-solving) but emphasizes the critical work of adapting them for cultural context—respecting family relationships, cultural protocols, and ways of yarning in community.

**Time & Capacity Realities**: As a volunteer facilitator running groups after hours, time is precious. Podcasts work best (can listen while exercising). Webinar recordings often saved but never watched. Micro-learning valued if it's practical and culturally informed.

**Platform Feature Priorities:**

- **Critical**: Mob-specific yarning spaces (private/invite-only for cultural safety)
- **Critical**: Meeting register (how community finds culturally safe support)
- **High value**: Podcasts featuring Aboriginal facilitators sharing their approaches
- **High value**: Facilitator profiles (if people control what's shared)
- **Yes with protocols**: Direct messaging (needs cultural boundaries)
- **Careful consideration**: Recognition/badges (must respect cultural protocols around acknowledgment)
- **Essential**: Clear moderation by people who understand cultural safety

**What Would Make Platform Transformative:**

- Ability to find and connect with other Aboriginal facilitators nationwide
- Stories from mob that show healing pathways and normalize experiences
- Resources adapted for cultural context, not just generic tools
- Safe spaces to yarn about the unique work of cultural adaptation
- Platform governance that centers cultural safety from the start

## **Cultural Safety - Non-Negotiables**

**"If it's culturally safe, that's the main thing for me."**

**What this means for platform design:**

- Cultural protocols and governance must be embedded from day one, not added later
- Moderation by people who understand cultural safety (not just content moderation)
- Clear ground rules about respectful language and cultural protocols visible from the start
- Reversible consent—people can edit or remove their contributions anytime
- Private/invite-only spaces for Mob-specific discussions (not everything is for public consumption)
- Facilitators control what personal information is shared in profiles
- Recognition systems must respect cultural protocols around acknowledgment (not everyone wants to be singled out or "big-noted")

**Critical quote:** *"We always frame it in ways that respect cultural protocols and family relationships."*

## **Facilitation Approach - Cultural Adaptation**

**How Danielle runs groups:**

- Uses evidence-based SMART tools (PIUS, CRAFT, problem-solving, five-step method)
- BUT: Constantly adapts them for cultural context
- Check-in format—what issues come up?
- New members given more time to yarn about what's happening
- Most common themes: communication, boundaries, self-care ("looking after Country in yourself")
- Integrates yarning and motivational interviewing adapted for community
- Frames tools in ways that respect cultural protocols and family relationships

**Resources referenced:**

- SMART Facilitators Guide
- Invitation to Change
- Five-step method
- PIUS (Points of Influence, Understanding, Support)
- CRAFT (Community Reinforcement and Family Training)
- Problem-solving tool
- ABC tool (occasionally)

**Learning approach:**

- Constantly listens to podcasts (suits morning exercise routine)
- Listens to American SMART Recovery Podcast
- Hasn't been able to attend facilitator workshops (time constraints)
- Uses meeting register to show participants where tools are
- Always learning, but needs to avoid getting stuck in comfort zone

## **Platform Features - Detailed Feedback**

### **🔴 CRITICAL - Must Have**

**Meeting Register**

*"That's the whole reason I got involved with SMART—to have a platform that could get the message out there that there's culturally safe groups out there and people can find them."*

- How mob find facilitators in the first place
- Needs to be more developed/visible
- Without this, community can't find support

**Mob-Specific Yarning Spaces (Topic Spaces for Aboriginal Facilitators)**

*"If there was a yarning space for Mob doing this work—that would be deadly."*

- Private/invite-only spaces for cultural discussions
- Safe space to discuss cultural adaptation of tools
- Connect with other Aboriginal facilitators across Australia
- Not everything is appropriate for public spaces
- Being online means connecting mob from anywhere

**Moderation with Cultural Understanding**

*"I absolutely think a moderator is important, especially someone who understands cultural safety."*

- Not just content moderation—cultural safety moderation
- Clear ground rules from the start
- No jargon, no disparaging terminology
- Understanding of cultural protocols

---

### **🟢 HIGH VALUE - Would Use**

**Podcasts Featuring Aboriginal Facilitators**

*"That would be deadly... especially featuring Aboriginal facilitators."*

- Suits lifestyle (can listen while exercising)
- Create Australian context (not just American podcast)
- Interview Aboriginal facilitators about their approaches
- Interview people behind the tools
- Share stories from mob going through similar experiences
- Helps people realize they're not alone
- Promotes facilitators and the work

**Facilitator Profiles**

*"It would be nice if it helps mob find facilitators they feel comfortable with."*

- Share background, where they work, what they stand for
- Helps community members find facilitators they connect with
- MUST be optional (not everyone wants details public)
- Respect for lived experience volunteers who want privacy
- Cultural protocols around visibility

**Micro-Courses/Refresher Learning**

*"There's a real danger I've got into my own comfort zone."*

- Help avoid getting stuck after years of facilitating
- Connect with other facilitators' approaches
- Make it easy (because everyone's volunteer)
- Doesn't need to be brand new—just conversations about existing resources
- Prompts about tools not used in a while (like mobile app)

**Resource Drops/Prompts**

- Monthly or bi-monthly resource sharing
- Could be existing resources, not just new ones
- Prompts facilitators to revisit tools
- Makes valuable content more visible

**Direct Messaging Other Facilitators**

- Would value connection with peers

- Respect for people's time and boundaries

**Recognition/Badges/Gamification**

- Not appropriate to single people out in some situations
- Must be done in ways that respect cultural ways of acknowledging people
- Needs careful design with cultural governance

**Participant-Facilitator Chat Channel**

*"I'd love it. The thing is capacity."*

- Would participate for brief questions/answers
- But capacity is the main issue
- Encourages people needing individual support to find local services
- Cultural safety protocols would need to be very clear

## **The Bigger Picture - Growing SMART for Mob**

**"It blows my mind how many mob don't know this resource exists."**

**Context:**

- Other organizations doing similar work (SHARC in Victoria, Family Drug Support nationally)
- SMART needs to be more visible in Aboriginal communities
- Evidence-based approach is valuable (not "tough love" or old rhetoric)
- Need to grow network of family and friends providers
- Importance of community members seeing: "There are other people going through the same stuff"

**What makes SMART valuable:**

- Evidence-based tools (SMART and Invitation to Change)
- Not relying on old AA/Al-Anon rhetoric
- Can be adapted for cultural context
- Provides framework when people don't know what else to do

**Platform opportunity:**

- Feature facilitators so community can find people they connect with
- "Oh, there's Danielle from Orana Haven, she's working with Aboriginal families, she sounds like she gets it"
- Or people find a different facilitator that suits them better—that's fine too
- Humanize the facilitators
- Make the work visible

## **Key Platform Design Principles (from this interview)**

1. **Cultural safety must be embedded, not added** - Governance, moderation, protocols from day one
2. **Enable connection between Aboriginal facilitators** - Currently invisible to each other; platform can change that
3. **Respect time constraints of volunteers** - Flexible formats, on-demand content, podcasts over webinars
4. **Choice and control over visibility** - Not everyone wants to be public; respect privacy and cultural protocols
5. **Celebrate and promote the work** - Feature facilitators, share stories, make the work visible to community
6. **Keep the meeting register strong** - This is how mob find support in the first place
7. **Create space for yarning about cultural adaptation** - This is skilled work that needs peer support

## **Questions for Co-Design**

1. **Cultural governance framework:** Who sets and maintains cultural safety protocols? How are they enforced? What's the process when something goes wrong?
2. **Mob-specific spaces:** Who can access? Invitation process? Self-identification considerations?
3. **Profile design:** What elements are mandatory vs. optional? How do we help mob find culturally safe facilitators while respecting privacy?
4. **Recognition systems:** How do we acknowledge contribution in ways that respect diverse cultural protocols around acknowledgment?
5. **Moderation team:** Who are they? What's their cultural competency? Training process?
6. **Content featuring Aboriginal facilitators:** How do we ensure reversible consent? Payment for contributed content? Intellectual property considerations?

## 🔍 2. Deep-dive (30–40-minute co-design conversation)

Use this when you’re running a proper discovery interview or small group.

### A. Warm-up

- “Tell me a bit about your SMART group — who attends, what’s working well?”
- “One word for how facilitation feels this week.”

### B. Walk through the *last time you needed help*

- “What triggered it?”
- “What did you try first?”
- “How long did it take to get what you needed?”
- “What was frustrating or surprising?”

### C. Explore the current toolkit

- “What tools, websites, chats, or documents do you use now?”
- “Which of these actually help — and which are a pain?”
- “Show me your current workaround for finding resources.”

### D. Test value of proposed features

Read each feature and ask “Would you use this *twice a week*?” and “Why / why not?”

- DMs with other facilitators
- 20-minute live webinar per month
- Micro-lessons (5–8-min refreshers)
- Resource Drop (one-page tools)
- Staff AMA or researcher Q&A
- Topic spaces (youth, justice, LGBTQIA+, Mob)
- Recognition (badges, shout-outs, opportunities)

### E. Cultural safety and governance

- “What ground rules would make you feel safe sharing here?”
- “How should moderators behave?”
- “If something goes wrong, what’s the right way to handle it?”

### F. Future vision

- “If this platform disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?”
- “If it works perfectly, what changes in your week?”

### G. Close

- “Would you join the pilot as a Founding Facilitator?”
- “Who else should we talk to?”
- Confirm consent: *“You can edit or withdraw anything you share later.”*
