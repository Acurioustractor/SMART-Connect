# Finding New Perspectives Through Photography: Chris's Journey

In November 2022, Chris walked into Clean Slate clinic seeking help for his relationship with alcohol. Like many others, he had used alcohol to "turn the volume down" on his mental health challenges for over 20 years. What he found there wasn't just medical support, but a pathway to connection through SMART Recovery meetings that would transform his relationship with both recovery and helping others.

## From Participant to Facilitator: A Journey of Connection

"When you're in the more challenging times of dependency, you can't easily visualize a better choice or a better life," Chris reflects. "You can't imagine that's even possible because you've gotten used to a pretty shitty situation for some time."

This insight would later inspire his participation in SMART's photography project, where participants were asked to capture images representing their recovery journey. For Chris, photography became a way to help others visualize possibilities they might not be able to see for themselves.

## The Power of Online Community

Chris's journey with SMART Recovery has been entirely online - a format he believes helps break down barriers for newcomers. "The reason I love online meetings is that it's a soft way of getting people comfortable with the situation," he explains. "You can start with your camera off, even just go into the chat and say 'hey, I just want to have a listen in.'"

This flexibility and focus on personal choice is what drew Chris to SMART Recovery over other programs. "What SMART and Clean Slate both do quite well is take that scientific evidence approach," he notes. "They're not abstinence-based models... you can just change the right now and figure out the rest of your life a bit later on."

## Capturing Recovery Through a Lens

When asked about his photographs for the project, Chris's face lights up talking about images of early morning beach visits with his dog, Presley. "That was a Sunday sunrise - I'd get up at like four in the morning, drive down to the Goldie, get on the beach before the sun's up... it's something I would just never have done in my previous life. I would have still been drinking from the night before and not been to bed yet."

These images represent more than just pretty scenery - they capture what Chris calls "a complete reversal" in lifestyle and the discovery of joy in simple moments. His dog Presley features prominently in the photos, representing a constant companion through both difficult times and recovery. "He's actually funnily enough the only one that knows what it was like, apart from me," Chris reflects.

## Beyond the Recovery Label

Now working at Clean Slate and facilitating SMART Recovery meetings, Chris brings unique insight into the phases of recovery. He describes the "pink cloud" effect - the initial months where everything feels new and positive - and the subsequent challenge of rediscovering identity beyond addiction.

"When you break up with your substance and you stop, that's when I started the relationship with myself," he shares. "I'm only a year and a half into knowing who I might be or what I want to be."

## The Power of Shared Experience

Chris's story and photographs remind us that recovery isn't just about what we leave behind - it's about what we discover along the way. Through the lens of his camera, he captures not just moments of serenity at sunrise, but evidence that change is possible, that joy can be found in simple moments, and that sometimes our most faithful companions through recovery have four legs and a wagging tail.

His images serve as beacons of hope for others in the SMART Recovery community, showing that beyond the challenges of dependency lies a world of new possibilities - even if those possibilities are as simple as watching the sun rise over the beach with your best friend.
