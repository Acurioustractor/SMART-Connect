Summary

### Meeting Purpose

Research interview conducted to gather facilitator feedback for developing a community platform for SMART Recovery facilitators. The project is being led by For the Curious Traveller in collaboration with SMART Recovery, with findings to be shared with April, Erin, and the SMART team.

### Current Facilitator Experience and Challenges

- Interviewee's team primarily facilitates SMART groups in prison settings (South Australia, some in Western Australia) and community-based programs
- Team was involved in rewriting the Inside Out prison-based SMART recovery program
- Facilitators rarely need help due to strong existing training and access to SMART team and communities of practice
- When support is needed, it's typically around navigating conflicts between harm minimization principles and corrections' zero-tolerance policies
- Current support mechanisms: internal team meetings, direct line managers, external clinical supervision
- Feedback on SMART programming is consistently positive, particularly for introducing participants to SMART resources and post-release options

### Tools and Resources Currently Used

- Facilitators access downloadable SMART tools and introduce participants to the SMART app
- Inside Out program includes more psychoeducational content and tools due to prison environment constraints
- Decisional balance tool is heavily used, sometimes leading to group debates
- SMART goals and achievement tracking are key resources
- In prison settings, physical printed materials are necessary due to lack of technology access
- Interviewee personally uses SMART tools for work-life balance
- Primary communication platform: Microsoft Teams (minimal use for program-specific discussions)

### Platform Feature Preferences

**Strongly Supported (Yes):**

- Direct messaging between facilitators for peer support, reflection, and addressing concerns like manipulation or collusion
- Topic-based spaces (youth, justice, etc.) - though noted these should remain interconnected as issues overlap
- Monthly 20-minute live webinars with replay/audio-only options to accommodate schedules
- Micro lessons/testing for knowledge refreshment
- Monthly resource drops to support ongoing professional development
- Mix of facilitator AMAs and research content, with emphasis on translating research into practical application
- General feed showing latest posts and comments
- Posts supporting video, images, and PDF attachments
- Comments and threading functionality
- Polls for gathering feedback
- User mentions/tagging
- Profile viewing and post reporting capabilities
- Online courses/micro lessons within the platform
- Live Zoom functionality with replays
- Interactive videos with Q&A capability
- Resource library
- Ability for users to edit and delete their own posts

**Mixed Response (Maybe):**

- Private spaces restricted to specific groups - concern about fragmenting community
- Likes/dislikes on posts
- Certificates for completed training
- Paid community manager for moderation
- AI moderation - concern it might block necessary conversations about drug use
- Separate participant community or integration of participants after significant time

**Features Most Likely to be Used Weekly:**

- Live learning sessions (lunch and learns/webinars)
- Resource library
- Peer discussion and support features

### Community Management and Culture

**Moderation Approach:**

- Combination of peer moderation (badge system), AI support, and staff oversight recommended
- For significant issues: hide message, contact individual privately, then potentially bring back publicly with context to show how it was handled
- Community self-moderation aligns with SMART principles
- Need clear ground rules/principles around fidelity to model, protecting participant confidentiality, and maintaining supportive environment

**Recognition and Engagement:**

- Strong support for peer recognition and gratitude systems
- Concern about creating hierarchy or single "source of truth" status
- Recognition should emphasize community contribution rather than status

**Ground Rules:**

- Clear principles needed for model fidelity and participant protection
- Important to have something to refer back to when addressing issues
- Ground rules should support open conversation while preventing glorification of drug use or other inappropriate content

### Topic Organization Considerations

- Challenge: Justice-involved groups naturally include LGBTI, Indigenous, and other identity considerations
- Community groups show some self-selection (justice-involved participants may dominate open groups)
- Facilitator perspective: benefit from cross-topic engagement as long as specialized conversations aren't overwhelmed
- Suggested compromise: single conversation space with topic tagging for threading

### Technology Context

- Team primarily uses Teams for internal communication
- Zoom used for one prison-based program with online facilitators
- Limited use of online community tools currently
- Staff mostly co-located in Adelaide
- Organization "still developing in the tech space"

### Historical Context and Value

- Team has delivered SMART for 10 years
- Previous management iteration lacked regular engagement with SMART team and training opportunities
- Current resources (tools, communities of practice, training) are highly valued
- Loss of current online community and tools would be most significant impact

## 🔍 2. Deep-dive (30–40-minute co-design conversation)

Use this when you’re running a proper discovery interview or small group.

### A. Warm-up

- “Tell me a bit about your SMART group — who attends, what’s working well?”
- “One word for how facilitation feels this week.”

### B. Walk through the *last time you needed help*

- “What triggered it?”
- “What did you try first?”
- “How long did it take to get what you needed?”
- “What was frustrating or surprising?”

### C. Explore the current toolkit

- “What tools, websites, chats, or documents do you use now?”
- “Which of these actually help — and which are a pain?”
- “Show me your current workaround for finding resources.”

### D. Test value of proposed features

Read each feature and ask “Would you use this *twice a week*?” and “Why / why not?”

- DMs with other facilitators
- 20-minute live webinar per month
- Micro-lessons (5–8-min refreshers)
- Resource Drop (one-page tools)
- Staff AMA or researcher Q&A
- Topic spaces (youth, justice, LGBTQIA+, Mob)
- Recognition (badges, shout-outs, opportunities)

### E. Cultural safety and governance

- “What ground rules would make you feel safe sharing here?”
- “How should moderators behave?”
- “If something goes wrong, what’s the right way to handle it?”

### F. Future vision

- “If this platform disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?”
- “If it works perfectly, what changes in your week?”
