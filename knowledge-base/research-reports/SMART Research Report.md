# SMART Connect Research Report
## Facilitator Community Discovery & Needs Analysis

**Report Date:** November 2025
**Research Period:** October-November 2025
**Total Interviews Conducted:** 15+ facilitators
**Survey Respondents:** 219 facilitators

---

## Executive Summary

This research synthesizes insights from 15+ in-depth facilitator interviews and survey data from 219 SMART Recovery facilitators across Australia and Aotearoa NZ. The findings reveal critical insights about facilitator needs, pain points, and opportunities for building a thriving peer community through the SMART Connect platform.

**Key Finding:** Facilitators are isolated, time-poor volunteers who crave peer connection and practical supportbut current engagement models don't match their reality. The opportunity lies in creating asynchronous, culturally safe spaces that respect their constraints while building genuine community.

---

## 1. The Facilitator Landscape

### Who Are SMART Recovery Facilitators?

**Diversity Across Settings:**
- Justice system facilitators
- Youth program facilitators
- LGBTQIA+ focused groups
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander community facilitators
- Family & Friends facilitators
- Corporate/NGO/government-embedded facilitators
- Volunteer facilitators (majority)

**Current Scale:**
- 349 active meetings (goal: 600 in 12 months, 1000 long-term)
- 232 meetings currently in Pathminder
- 114 monthly active portal users (stagnant since January 2024)
- Only 19% login 2+ times per month
- Only 7% login 4+ times per month

### The Volunteer Reality

**Time Constraints** (Most Critical Barrier):
- Facilitators run groups **after hours** as volunteer work
- No time during work hours for professional development
- "Wishful thinking" about watching webinar recordingsnever happens
- Need solutions that fit into existing routines (e.g., podcasts while exercising)

**Isolation Challenge:**
- Facilitators operate in "own little worlds"
- Don't know other facilitators doing similar work
- No easy way to connect with peers (especially culturally-specific peers)
- Risk of getting stuck in "comfort zone" without peer connection
- Many don't even know about existing Facebook group

## 2. Current Pain Points

### What Facilitators Need Support With

**Top Support Needs (Survey Data, n=219):**
1. **Confidence / More Practice Sessions:** 62.56%
2. **Co-Facilitation:** 47.03%
3. **Help Finding Participants / Advertising:** 17.81%
4. **Help Registering with SMART AU:** 12.33%
5. **Help Setting Up Zoom:** 6.85%

### When Facilitators Actually Need Help

**Common Support Triggers:**
- Technical issues (Zoom backend, facilitator portal navigation)
- Need for debrief/reflection after difficult sessions
- Policy changes (e.g., verbal consent procedures)
- Cultural adaptation of tools for specific communities
- Finding other facilitators for coverage or co-facilitation

### Current Workarounds

**What Facilitators Do Now:**
- Email staff directly for tech support
- Create personal Google Docs/Slides for meeting resources
- Occasionally participate in international online groups (Allies in Recovery, American groups)
- Build WhatsApp groups for their specific participants (not SMART-sanctioned)
- Save webinar recordings with good intentions (never watch)
- Listen to American SMART Recovery Podcast (not Australian content)

**Medium Priority (40-60% interest):**

### Content Format Preferences

**What Works:**
- **Podcasts** (can listen while exercising, suits lifestyle)
- **Micro-lessons** (5-8 min refreshers, practical)
- **On-demand resources** (not just live sessions)
- **Quick prompts/reminders** about existing tools
- **Text-based discussions** (asynchronous)

**What Doesn't Work:**
- Live webinars (time constraints, never watch replays)
- Time-intensive professional development (can't take time off)
- Complex onboarding (need <2 minutes for GPs, same principle applies)
- Lengthy courses (unless truly valuable)

## 4. Cultural Safety: Non-Negotiable for Aboriginal Facilitators

### Insights from Aboriginal Facilitator Research (Danielle Caruana Interview)

**Critical Quote:**
*"If it's culturally safe, that's the main thing for me."*

### What Cultural Safety Means in Platform Design

**1. Embedded from Day One (Not Added Later):**
- Cultural protocols and governance must be foundational
- Moderation by people who understand cultural safety (not just content moderation)
- Clear ground rules about respectful language and cultural protocols
- Reversible consentpeople can edit or remove contributions anytime

**2. Private/Invite-Only Mob Spaces:**
- Not everything is for public consumption
- Safe spaces to yarn about cultural adaptation work
- Private discussions about adapting SMART tools for cultural context
- Respecting family relationships and cultural protocols

**3. Control Over Visibility:**
- Facilitators control what personal information is shared in profiles
- Recognition systems must respect cultural protocols around acknowledgment
- Not everyone wants to be singled out or "big-noted"
- Optional participation in public features

**4. The Isolation Problem for Aboriginal Facilitators:**

*"It'd be good to know who else is out there doing this work with Aboriginal communities."*

- Currently don't know other Aboriginal facilitators
- No way to connect with peers doing similar cultural work
- Would be "deadly" to see profiles of other facilitators working with Mob
- Need platform to make these connections visible and accessible

**5. Cultural Adaptation is Skilled Work:**
- Use evidence-based SMART tools (PIUS, CRAFT, problem-solving, five-step method)
- BUT: Constantly adapt them for cultural context
- Frame tools in ways that respect cultural protocols and family relationships
- Integrate yarning and motivational interviewing adapted for community
- Most common themes: communication, boundaries, self-care ("looking after Country in yourself")

## 5. Key Insights by Facilitator Type

### LGBTQIA+ Facilitators (Ambika Scott Jodrell)

**What Works:**
- Specific spaces for LGBTQIA+ facilitators to connect
- Community of practice for facilitators running Rainbow meetings
- Tagging system to help people find relevant content
- Preference for "group agreements" over "ground rules" (language matters)

**Concerns:**
- Gamification/badges feel inauthentic
- AI moderation has "ick factor" (but understands resource constraints)
- Anonymous posting riskyaccountability important
- Profile creation shouldn't be burdensome

**Strong Interest In:**
- Webinars where they can contribute (willing to run one)
- Connecting with other LGBTQIA+ facilitators doing this work
- Live sessions with recordings (asynchronous access critical)

### Family & Friends Facilitators (Mark Powell)

**Unique Needs:**
- Different content focus (communication, boundaries, self-care vs addiction tools)
- Competition with other organizations (SHARC in Victoria, Family Drug Support nationally)
- Need to be visible so families can find culturally safe, geographically accessible support

**What Would Drive Engagement:**
- Australian SMART podcast featuring facilitators and tool experts
- Ability to find and connect with other Family & Friends facilitators
- Meeting register improvements (critical for discoverability)
- Micro-courses for skill refresh (avoid comfort zone trap)

**Time Constraints:**
- Podcast format suits lifestyle (morning exercise)
- Saves webinars but never watches them
- Needs simple, quick touchpoints

### Justice/Organizational Facilitators

**Context:**
- Facilitating within larger organizational structures
- May have more resources but also more constraints
- Need evidence-based approach to justify to stakeholders
- Value SMART for evidence base (vs "tough love" or AA/Al-Anon rhetoric)

## 6. Platform Engagement: What's Working & What's Not

### Current Portal Performance (Facilitator Hub)

**User Base:**
- 349 active facilitators
- 114 monthly active users (32% of facilitators)
- **96 users (19%) login 2+ times per month**
- **36 users (7%) login 4+ times per month**

**Problem Diagnosis:**
- Number stagnant since January 2024
- Lack of dynamic, engaging content
- People login once after training, never return
- V2 priorities don't align with SRAU strategic goals
- Portal risks becoming organizational burden rather than asset

### What Would Drive Engagement?

**Based on Facilitator Feedback:**

1. **Authentic Stories & Real People:**
   - Facilitators sharing their approaches
   - Stories from mob/participants showing healing pathways
   - Normalize experiences ("I'm not alone in this journey")
   - Feature facilitators so community can find people they connect with

2. **Peer-to-Peer Value Creation:**
   - Facilitators helping each other solve challenges
   - Resource sharing (session plans, reflection tools, peer support scripts)
   - Coverage requests and co-facilitation matching
   - Q&A where peers respond (not just staff)

3. **Practical, Actionable Content:**
   - Monthly/bi-monthly resource drops with prompts
   - Doesn't need to be newconversations about existing tools
   - Reminders about tools not used in a while (like mobile app)
   - Cultural adaptation resources

4. **Connection Pathways:**
   - Facilitator profiles to see who's out there
   - Topic-specific spaces (Youth, Justice, LGBTQIA+, Mob, Family & Friends)
   - Direct messaging with cultural boundaries
   - Clear moderation that maintains safety

## 7. Community Models: What Facilitators Recognize

### Existing Communities Facilitators Use

**Facebook:**
- Existing SMART facilitator Facebook group (underutilized)
- Many don't know it exists
- Those aware say it's "quiet" and not engaging
- Concerns about Facebook not being appropriate for everyone

**Calisthenics/Sports Communities:**
- Some facilitators mention other community platforms (clubs, sports)
- Interest in seeing what works in other contexts

**Lack of SMART-Specific Models:**
- Generally not using community platforms actively
- Instagram/Facebook daily (conditioning) but not for professional community
- No habit of logging into spaces specifically for facilitator connection

### Reluctance Around Participant Communities

**Strong Concerns from Facilitators:**

*From Ambika: "I'd be concerned about SMART putting forward something that blurs the lines between self-management and accountability."*

**Why Facilitators Are Cautious:**
- Conflicts with SMART philosophy of self-management
- Blurs boundaries between facilitation and sponsorship/accountability
- Risk management concerns if participants share concerning content
- Already adequate meeting access (online meetings almost every night for some demographics)
- Reddit (15,000 members) and Discord communities already exist (unmoderated, "cowboy-like")

**When It Might Work:**
- Brief Q&A with clear capacity boundaries
- Cultural safety protocols very clear
- For demographics with limited meeting access
- If framed as peer support (not SMART-as-sponsor)

## 9. Synthesis: Design Principles for SMART Connect

### Based on All Research, These Principles Should Guide Platform Development:

**1. Respect Time Constraints**
- Asynchronous-first design
- Micro-content (<5-8 minutes)
- Mobile-friendly for on-the-go access
- Podcast/audio format options
- No mandatory live sessions

**2. Enable Peer Connection**
- Facilitator profiles (with privacy controls)
- Topic-specific spaces (Youth, Justice, LGBTQIA+, Mob, Family & Friends)
- Direct messaging (with boundaries)
- Co-facilitation matching
- Coverage request boards

**3. Create Genuine Value Through Peer-to-Peer Support**
- Discussion boards (78% want this!)
- Q&A where facilitators help each other
- Resource sharing (templates, session plans, tools)
- Real stories from facilitators
- Recognition that facilitators are experts

**4. Embed Cultural Safety from Day One**
- Private/invite-only spaces for Mob
- Cultural governance framework
- Moderation by people who understand cultural safety
- Reversible consent always
- Control over visibility and what's shared

**5. Make Content Dynamic & Engaging**
- Regular news and updates (70% want this)
- Monthly resource drops with prompts
- Facilitator spotlight features
- Participant stories (with consent)
- Australian podcast featuring facilitators

**6. Build on Existing Habits**
- Integration with tools they already use (email, meeting register)
- Notifications that work (not spam)
- Simple onboarding (<2 minutes like GP goal)
- Leverage existing relationships (staff, peers they know)

**7. Avoid Pitfalls**
- Don't rely on gamification (9% interest in badges)
- Don't require anonymous spaces (facilitators want accountability)
- Don't force live attendance (time constraints)
- Don't create participant communities (philosophy conflicts)
- Don't add complexity (volunteer fatigue is real)

## 10. Opportunity Areas

### Immediate Wins (Next 6 Months)

**1. Launch Discussion Board**
- Highest demand (78%)
- Enables peer-to-peer support
- Creates content through facilitator contributions
- Can be moderated by trained facilitators

**2. Facilitator Profiles & Discovery**
- Help facilitators find each other
- Especially critical for Aboriginal facilitators
- Optional participation
- Privacy controls

**3. News & Updates Hub**
- 70% want this
- Regular content keeps people coming back
- Can include resource drops, policy updates, facilitator spotlights
- Low barrier to entry

**4. Topic-Specific Spaces**
- 52% want special interest groups
- Critical for cultural safety (Mob spaces)
- Enables niche connection (LGBTQIA+, Youth, Family & Friends, Justice)
- Private/invite-only options

**5. Australian SMART Podcast**
- Suits facilitator lifestyles
- Feature facilitators sharing approaches
- Interview tool developers
- Share participant stories (with consent)
- Asynchronous learning

### Medium-Term Opportunities (6-12 Months)

**1. Communities of Practice**
- Structured peer learning
- Topic-specific (e.g., LGBTQIA+ facilitators, Aboriginal facilitators)
- Regular touchpoints (monthly)
- Both async discussion + optional live sessions

**2. Resource Templates**
- Session plans
- Reflection tools
- Peer support scripts
- Cultural adaptation guides
- Facilitator-created, facilitator-shared

**3. Micro-Learning Library**
- 5-8 minute refreshers
- Practical, skills-based
- Optional certificates (not badges)
- Helps facilitators avoid "comfort zone trap"

**4. Co-Facilitation Matching**
- Based on location, demographic focus, availability
- Addresses #2 support need (47% want co-facilitation)
- Reduces isolation
- Builds skills through peer learning

### Long-Term Vision (12+ Months)

**1. Meeting Register Integration**
- Critical for discoverability
- Help community find culturally safe facilitators
- Showcase diversity of offerings
- Support 600-1000 meeting goal

**2. AI-Powered Support (With Caution)**
- 24/7 chatbot for basic questions
- Cultural safety guardrails
- Human escalation pathways
- Transparent about what AI can/can't do

**3. Participant Support Pathway**
- Brief Q&A with clear capacity boundaries
- Cultural safety protocols
- Separate from facilitator community
- Managed expectations (not accountability/sponsorship)

## 12. Metrics That Matter

### Don't Just Track LoginsTrack Value Creation

**Engagement Metrics:**
- **Discussion board activity** (posts, replies, helpful votes)
- **Peer connections made** (DMs, co-facilitation matches, coverage requests filled)
- **Resource sharing** (uploads, downloads, facilitator-created content)
- **Return visitors** (2+ sessions per month, 4+ sessions per month)
- **Time to value** (how quickly do new users find something useful?)

**Community Health Metrics:**
- **Diversity of voices** (are all demographics represented?)
- **Cultural safety incidents** (and resolution time)
- **Facilitator retention** (do engaged portal users run meetings longer?)
- **Peer support success** (questions answered by other facilitators vs staff)

**Strategic Alignment Metrics:**
- **Meeting growth** (does platform support 600-1000 meeting goal?)
- **Facilitator confidence** (self-reported in surveys)
- **Isolation reduction** (facilitators report feeling connected)
- **Skills development** (facilitators trying new approaches learned from peers)

3. News & updates hub
4. Topic-specific spaces (including private Mob space)

5. Micro-learning library

**Priority 3: Scale & Integrate (12+ months)**
1. Meeting register integration
2. Mobile app with community features
3. AI-powered support (with human oversight)
4. Participant support pathway (carefully)

### For Content Strategy

**Week 1-4: Seed the Community**
- Staff post discussion prompts
- Feature facilitators weekly
- Share practical resources
- Celebrate wins

**Month 2-3: Activate Power Users**
- Identify engaged facilitators
- Invite to contribute content
- Spotlight their work
- Create peer moderators

**Month 4+: Community-Led**
- Facilitators drive discussions
- Staff amplifies and supports
- Regular rhythms established
- Self-sustaining peer support

### For Cultural Safety

**Immediate:**
- Co-design governance framework with Aboriginal facilitators
- Establish moderation team with cultural competency
- Create private Mob space with invitation process
- Train all moderators on cultural safety protocols

**Ongoing:**
- Regular consultation with Aboriginal facilitators
- Cultural safety audits of content and features
- Continuous improvement based on feedback
- Aboriginal leadership in decision-making

## 15. Risks & Mitigation

### Key Risks

**1. Low Adoption**
- *Risk:* Facilitators don't engage, platform becomes ghost town
- *Mitigation:* Seed with valuable content, activate power users early, integrate with existing touchpoints (email, training)

**2. Cultural Safety Violations**
- *Risk:* Platform not safe for Aboriginal facilitators or other marginalized groups
- *Mitigation:* Co-design governance, cultural competency in moderation, private spaces, clear protocols

**3. Volunteer Fatigue**
- *Risk:* Adding another platform to manage overwhelms already time-poor facilitators
- *Mitigation:* Make it genuinely valuable (not just another obligation), asynchronous-first, integrate with existing workflows

**4. Misalignment with Strategic Goals**
- *Risk:* Platform doesn't support 600-1000 meeting growth goal
- *Mitigation:* Regular check-ins with SRAU leadership, tie features to strategic metrics, pivot based on data

**5. Competition with Existing Solutions**
- *Risk:* Facilitators prefer Facebook group or other platforms
- *Mitigation:* Offer unique value (cultural safety, targeted features), don't force migration, allow cross-posting

---

## Conclusion

The opportunity is clear: **SMART Recovery facilitators are isolated, time-poor volunteers who crave peer connection and practical support**. The current portal isn't meeting their needs because it lacks dynamic, engaging content and doesn't enable the peer-to-peer value creation that would make it indispensable.

The path forward is equally clear: **Create asynchronous, culturally safe spaces that enable facilitators to help each other, share resources, and build genuine community**all while respecting their time constraints and volunteer reality.

This isn't about building a platform. It's about **growing the heart of SMART Recovery: the facilitator community**.

The facilitators are ready. The strategic alignment is there. The research shows the way.

Now it's time to build.
